Backplate fan for the RTX 3090

denguinho-server · GDDR6X junction fix
drawn 2026-08-19 · sheet 1/1

Half of the 3090's GDDR6X chips sit on the back of the PCB, cooled only by the passive backplate. On this card we measured 98 °C memory junction during sustained decode while the core sat at 63 °C — with the card's own fans at 0 RPM (they watch the core, not the memory). A plain fan blowing on the backplate is worth ≈8–12 °C.

C · Steps

  1. Pick the fan 92 or 120 mm. PWM (4-pin) if a motherboard header is reachable; any spare case fan works.
  2. Find the hot spot Run a long generation, power down, touch the backplate — or trust Fig. B (behind the die, 11–13 cm from the bracket).
  3. Set the gap Don't press the fan flat on the plate — leave 5–15 mm so air can escape sideways. Stick 4 rubber/silicone pads (or folded electrical tape stacks) on the fan's corners.
  4. Mount it Easiest first: the card hangs backplate-up, so the fan can rest on its pads by gravity. Secure it with two zip ties through the fan's corner holes to anything nearby (power cables' comb, a case strut, the backplate's fin gaps). See options D.
  5. Wire it Motherboard chassis-fan header → BIOS: fixed 70–80 % (the board can't see VRAM temp, so don't use a temperature curve). No header? SATA/Molex adapter = full speed, fine for a server.
  6. Route the cable away from the GPU's own fan intakes below the card and the CPU cooler.

D · Mounting options, best → last resort

Gravity + zip ties

Fan lies on rubber pads, two zip ties for security. 5 min, free, zero risk. The default.

Slot-cover bracket

Bend/drill a spare PCI slot cover or buy a cheap "GPU fan bracket"; screws into the case's slot screws, holds the fan above the plate rigidly.

Magnet feet

Screw M4 magnet standoffs into the fan corners; sticks to any steel case wall near the card. Not to the backplate itself (usually aluminium).

Twist ties / wire

Through the backplate's fin slots if your model has them. Avoid blocking the slots that exist for airflow.
Don't: block the card's own three fans (they're on the underside — a floor-mounted intake below the card matters more than the top), rest anything conductive on bare PCB edges, or cover backplate vent cut-outs. The fan pushes onto the plate — flipping it to suck is measurably worse for hotspots.

E · Verify — this is the acceptance test

Same workload before and after (a long generation, ≥10 min), read the memory junction directly (nvidia-smi cannot see it): echo <sudo-pw> | sudo -S timeout 10 /tmp/gddr6/gddr6-bin # prints VRAM junction °C Baseline on this card: 96–98 °C steady-state with the card's own fans at 0 RPM; forcing those fans to 100 % measured 92 °C. Success for the backplate fan = steady-state junction in the low-to-mid 80s combined with the fan-curve fix. Anything under ~90 °C also secures the memory-overclock headroom (+5 % decode, silicon already validated to +1250 MHz with bit-identical output).
